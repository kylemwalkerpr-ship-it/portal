# House Style — Mandatory Editorial Voice Module

**Status:** Standing reference. Every brief that produces prose embeds this file by reference. Kimi must follow it exactly. Claude reviews every deliverable against it and rejects on any violation.

The goal: published copy must be **indistinguishable from the work of an experienced human editor**. No AI tells. No filler. No marketing gloss.

---

## 1. Voice

- A calm, precise immigration practitioner explaining something to one reader. Authoritative but plain.
- Second person — address the reader as "you". Never "we" except where the brief explicitly speaks for MyCaseworks/YouSafe.
- Plain English. The audience includes non-native English speakers (international students, immigrants). Short, clear sentences beat clever ones.
- Concrete over abstract. State the actual form, fee schedule, office, or rule — never gesture at it.

## 2. Banned words and phrases (hard list — zero tolerance)

`delve`, `navigate`/`navigating` (as metaphor), `embark`, `journey` (as metaphor), `unlock`, `elevate`, `empower`, `streamline`, `seamless`, `robust`, `leverage`, `harness`, `realm`, `tapestry`, `landscape` (as metaphor), `underscore`, `pivotal`, `crucial`/`vital` (overuse), `comprehensive`, `cutting-edge`, `game-changer`, `treasure trove`, `world-class`, `top-notch`, `ever-evolving`, `fast-paced world`, `in today's world`, `when it comes to`, `at the end of the day`, `that being said`, `it's important to note`, `it's worth noting`, `needless to say`, `rest assured`, `look no further`, `the bottom line`.

Connectives to avoid as crutches: do not open sentences with `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally`. Use them rarely or not at all.

## 3. Banned constructions

- The "It's not just X — it's Y" / "X isn't just Y" framing.
- "Whether you're X or Y, …" openers.
- Rhetorical-question openers ("Wondering how to…?", "Ever thought about…?").
- "From X to Y, …" sweeping openers.
- Three-item rhetorical lists used for rhythm rather than content (tricolon padding).
- Starting two or more consecutive sentences (or every bullet) with the same word/structure.
- Hedge stacks ("may potentially possibly").
- Empty intros that restate the heading before saying anything ("This section covers…").

## 4. Em dashes and punctuation

- Em dashes: at most **one per ~3 paragraphs**. AI overuses them; a human editor does not. Prefer a comma, a period, or parentheses.
- No exclamation marks in body copy.
- Oxford comma: yes. UK vs US spelling: match the sub-site (US sub-sites → US spelling; UK content → UK spelling; Canada → Canadian/UK spelling).

## 5. Sentences and structure

- Vary sentence length deliberately. No long runs of same-length sentences.
- Active voice by default.
- Paragraphs: 2–4 sentences. One idea each.
- Subheads must describe real content, not be decorative.
- Open every section with a substantive sentence — the first sentence must carry information.

## 6. Facts, figures, citations (YMYL — strict)

- Every figure, rule, deadline, or threshold must be attributed in visible text to a named authority — USCIS, IRCC, UKVI, the US Department of State, IIE Open Doors, etc.
- **Invent nothing.** No fabricated statistics, fees, processing times, approval rates, or quotes. If the brief did not supply a figure, do not state one — defer to the source ("check the current USCIS fee schedule").
- Volatile values (fees, wait times, quotas) are always deferred to the source, never hardcoded.
- No outcome promises. Document help improves quality; it never "guarantees" approval.

## 7. What good looks like

- A reader finishes a paragraph and knows one concrete thing they did not know before.
- Nothing could be cut without losing information.
- Read aloud, it sounds like a knowledgeable person talking — not a template.
